# Composition Decision Framework

Read this before proposing a crop. Composition is a hierarchy decision, not a mandatory rule-of-thirds overlay.

## Analyze in This Order

1. Identify the primary subject and the visual event.
2. Identify story-supporting context: companion, environment, horizon, motion direction, weather, table setting, or architecture.
3. Scan each edge for protected content: face, hand, person, animal, label, text, collectible, product edge, meaningful object, or location cue.
4. Evaluate frame balance: dead space, edge distraction, headroom, lead room, horizon placement, verticals, symmetry, and visual weight.
5. Decide whether full frame is stronger. If yes, export no crop and do not invent a reason to trim.
6. If crop is justified, choose one ratio and one crop rectangle. Do not propose a menu of arbitrary social-media ratios.

## Ratio Rules

| Ratio | Strong use | Warning |
|---|---|---|
| Original | Documentary, group, or context-dependent image; already balanced frame | Do not crop merely because a popular ratio exists |
| 3:2 / 4:3 | Everyday record, travel, interior, or environmental scene | Protect story context at edges |
| 16:9 | Sky, road direction, city line, broad horizontal movement | Do not cut meaningful vertical subject information |
| 4:5 | Vertical portrait, food, or single object with safely removable top/bottom space | Do not cut hands, feet, text, or plating context |
| 1:1 | Symmetry, an intimate single subject, or a clear centered relationship | Do not flatten a scene that needs lateral space |
| 9:16 | Vertical architecture, standing subject, or explicit mobile-story delivery | Do not use only because the image is vertical |

## Geometry Rules

Straighten a visibly tilted horizon or a vertical cue that should be upright. Apply perspective correction only when converging verticals distract from the subject. State the intended correction. Do not rotate simply to create dynamism, and do not use perspective correction to alter a person or object shape.

## Auto-Execution Gate

Auto-execute one crop only when all are true: the subject is clear; the new ratio has a stated purpose; all removed edges are clearly non-meaningful; no face, person, hand, animal close-up, text, label, collectible, or documentary context is affected; and there is no uncertainty about the geometry.

Otherwise show or state the crop contract and request a simple approval. If the user does not respond, keep the full frame rather than guess.

## Output Language

Use this concise explanation before export:

> I will crop to **[ratio]** from `[left, top, right, bottom]` to place **[subject]** at **[placement]** and remove **[non-meaningful edge area]**. I will keep **[protected context]** and apply **[none / rotation / perspective correction]** only.

## Forbidden Operations

Do not apply color, exposure, contrast, saturation, skin, texture, blur, sharpening, content removal, object replacement, expansion, generative fill, or semantic editing. If the user asks for those, route them to a different photo-editing workflow after completing or declining the composition task.
